01  /  Daraz · Design strategy & rebranding

Rebranding Bangladesh.

Leading eight designers through Daraz’s regional rebrand: 200–300 key visuals in about two months, adapted for Bangladesh.

8designers on the Bangladesh team
200–300key visuals (approximate)
~2 monthsrollout window (approximate)
2021–22Head of Design (Marketing)
My role

I led an eight-person Bangladesh marketing design team and served as the local design point of contact while Daraz rolled out a regional rebrand. The identity was set across the region; my job was to make it work in Bangladesh, across marketing, campaigns and selected app touchpoints.

How the team worked

Work ran on a Trello board. Three designers handled offline work, three handled online work, and urgent requests came to me and my associate team lead. That split kept campaign deadlines from competing with the larger rollout.

Decisions
  • Shape language: disagree and commit. The regional shape language leaned toward Singapore and Malaysia and did not fit Bangladesh’s cultural diversity. No one on the local team, including me, agreed with it. We committed anyway, because consistency across countries mattered more than local preference.
  • Trade-off on scope. With a tight deadline, a few secondary assets were delayed so the key visuals could ship on time.
  • Customer reaction. Customers were initially unsettled by the new layout and design style. Over time the team and the customers came on board.
Evidence

Figures are approximate and come from my own records. Sales, conversion and brand-lift changes are not attributed to this work.

03  /  OLab · Online and offline learning

Starting with the questions students actually asked.

I designed the learning experience and spoke with students, mentors and business stakeholders about cost, guidance and relevant skills.

8focus-group findings
33learner survey responses
~70%course non-completion (my estimate)
Research

Focus groups with students, mentors and industry experts changed the original brief. Eight findings stood out:

Mentor credibilityCost barrierInformal career adviceCourse non-completionMid-career skill gapAfter-course support gapOutcome-led expectationsExpert recruitment

The non-completion figure is an estimate recalled from the sessions, not measured data.

Survey

33 learners described previous learning and mentor choices. 22 of 33 had taken an online course in the previous year, 22 of 29 pointed to a local institute or mentor, and 18 of 30 rated a course they had taken 4 or 5 out of 5. A small sample, useful as direction only.

Direction
Qualified mentors

Assess relevant professional work and teaching ability before assigning mentors.

Accessible training

Pricing tiers, shorter formats and clear costs for different budgets.

Support to completion

Progress dashboards and lesson access, with check-ins where delivered.

Career pathways

Connect training to portfolio work, practical projects and mid-career upskilling.

04  /  SWAP · Marketplace UX redesign

Buy. Sell. Swap, with clarity.

A product and visual-system redesign for buying, selling and exchanging devices, 20 March to 30 June 2023.

7buy steps in the earlier journey
10+sell steps in the earlier journey
3journeys: buy, sell, swap
The problem

SWAP served B2B and C2C users buying, selling and exchanging gadgets, bikes, cars and accessories. The earlier design made buying, selling and evaluating exchange offers harder than it needed to be. The business also reported a sales decline, without time-series data available to the design team.

Insight

One marketplace, three intentions. Someone buying a phone, selling an old device or checking a swap offer needs a different next step, so the information architecture separated those jobs.

Research

Over 40 people were approached; 33 answered two questions and 29 answered the one about used electronics, with 75.9% interested. Four needs came through: product selection, a usable interface, seller trust and authenticity, and budget-friendly options.

What I produced

User flows, a FigJam mind map, wireframes, a blue visual system, and high-fidelity screens for sell, buy, swap, payment and order confirmation. Conversion, selling speed and sales recovery are not reported in this case study.

05  /  Offshore Clipping · Brand and web experience

Making a service feel trustworthy.

A brand and website redesign for a professional photo-editing service, with clearer discovery, pricing and communication. March to June 2023.

ImageOffshore Clipping: website homepageHero banner, wide · 1600×900
ImageBrand guidelineLogo, colour, type · 1200×900
ImageCommunicationContact and specialist screens · 1200×900
ImagePricing and quoteCalculator concept · 1200×900
The challenge

Retention and conversion were concerns. The site had a dated brand, disorganized content, weak communication and no clear pricing, and visitors had to trust the service with their work before they could see what it offered.

Insights
Branding

Modern identity and recognizable visual standards.

Content

Clear descriptions, proof of work and organized services.

Contact

Multiple ways to reach a real support person.

Pricing

Upfront estimates, turnaround options and next steps.

What I designed

A brand guideline with a blue-and-cyan logo and Montserrat typography, a homepage built around services, proof and a clear route to a quote, contact and specialist-selection concepts, and a real-time pricing calculator with an editable quote view. The calculator is a concept design with sample values.
